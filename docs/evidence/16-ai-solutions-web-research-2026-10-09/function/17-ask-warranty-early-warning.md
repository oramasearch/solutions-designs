## Named deployments with quantified results

- **Sub-Zero Group + SAS Field Quality Analytics:** The system analyzed technician notes, warranty claims, call-center records, and product data for early warning and root-cause analysis. Sub-Zero reported a **50% reduction in service incident rates over five years** and said analytics helped uncover endemic problems **four to five months sooner**.[8]
- **Regional automobile manufacturer + SAS:** SAS reports issue-identification time fell **70%**, from an average **174 days to 52 days**—a reduction of 122 days. The manufacturer reported **more than $2 million in savings in the first six months**.[1][4]
- **Luxury appliance manufacturer + SAS:** SAS says automated text-based claims coding and an early-warning process cut service incident rates in half; a company executive also reported **double-digit service-incident improvements** and year-over-year warranty-cost decreases, but no dollar or percentage cost figure is given.[1][4]
- **Tavant, unnamed multinational manufacturer:** A Tavant case describes a centralized warranty system used to track parts failures and flag fraudulent claims. It reports early failure detection and better warranty-cost management, but **does not publish a quantified fraud-detection rate or cost reduction**.[2]
- **Tavant, large engine OEM (unnamed):** A recent Tavant-published account says the OEM centralized **98,000+ claims**; rules auto-approved **over 83%**, and claim cycle time fell from weeks to hours. These are claims-processing outcomes, not a measured defect-detection or warranty-cost reduction.[7]

## Other evidence and limits

SAS advertises potential—not guaranteed customer results—of **up to 20% lower warranty costs** and **up to 70% shorter resolution time**. Its public material also describes a global heavy-equipment deployment that integrated data across brands and enabled early warning, but does not provide a quantified outcome.[1][4]

For **Daimler Truck North America**, Tavant lists a named warranty-transformation case study, but the surfaced summary gives no metrics. The search results did not substantiate quantified deployments for **Pitstop, Mize, Bosch, John Deere, Navistar, or Whirlpool**; that does not establish that none exist.[5]

## Takeaway for evaluating vendors

The clearest disclosed early-warning measures are **days saved to identify an issue** and **service-incident reductions**. Public examples provide much less evidence for recall avoidance, confirmed fraud dollars, or net warranty-cost savings. Ask vendors for customer references and baseline-versus-after data on detection latency, claims cost per unit, campaign/recall spend, false alerts, and fraud confirmed—not just automated claim-processing rates.
