# 12 — For investors: the bet, the moat, the wedge

**Logline:** Every company is trying to turn its data into intelligence, and almost none can do it fast. Orama Zetta sells the velocity — and the engine underneath compounds into a moat.

**For:** investors and strategic partners.

---

**The bet.** The scarce resource in the AI era isn't models — those are commoditizing — it's the ability to turn a company's own data into correct, governed, deployed intelligence, fast. Today that takes scarce engineers and months. Orama Zetta collapses it to minutes and puts it in a GUI. The category is **auto-ANI for the enterprise** — automated, production-grade artificial *narrow* intelligence, optimized for cost, governance, and personalized accuracy. Everyone is chasing AGI; what a company actually needs is auto-ANI. Framed for buyers, that reads as "self-deploying Databricks" / "Railway for private-cloud data + AI," and the wedge in is **velocity**.

**The wedge + the motion.** We don't boil the ocean. We land one enterprise on one painful, high-value use case — Motive, on churn — prove the loop, and then run it again. The first deal is the hard one; each subsequent one is an order of magnitude less work, and the champion introduces us to their network. A handful of six-figure enterprise deals makes the company self-sustaining and is the proof that the motion repeats.

**The moat.** Three compounding assets. First, the **engine** (Orama Zetta) — the world's first *adaptive* database, built in C++ with use-case-specific, fused GPU kernels for native speed and transformational cost profiles. Data-locality + tiered storage means we're not just easier, we're *cheaper and faster* — structural, not a feature — and it trains models and pipelines (typically an ensemble), not a single rented model. Second, **true hybrid**: one cluster spans every vendor and architecture — CPU, NVIDIA CUDA, AMD ROCm, Apple MLX, Vulkan/SYCL, GCP TPU, AWS Neuron; macOS/Linux/Windows; x86/ARM; AWS/GCP/Azure/Oracle Cloud/Kubernetes/bare metal — giving customers ultimate control over cost, flexibility, and resilience, and leaving them un-exposed to any single vendor's outages or degradations. Third, **experience**: every model, pipeline, and graph the platform builds, and every run it executes, is grounded data that makes the next one better — a flywheel a hand-built competitor can't match because they re-derive each problem from scratch. Pedigree underwrites all three: Orama Zetta comes from the creator of OpenShift.

**The honest part.** The engine is early — clustering and replication are real; the tiered storage, compute-near-data, and indexing are the build ahead. We're explicit about that internally, and the roadmap is sequenced to deliver value on what exists while the moat-deepening pieces land. The cost-advantage figures are hypotheses until measured — we don't quote them as fact.
