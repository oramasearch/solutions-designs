The strongest publicly documented production examples are in **food-waste measurement and forecasting**. For overbooking, staffing, housekeeping, crew recovery, and ancillary offers, the available sources here do not support a reliable, quantified ROI claim; those areas need more specific evidence before naming winners.

| Use case | Company / deployment | Measured outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Hotel F&B demand and prep forecasting** | Winnow Foresight at Hilton Frankfurt Airport and Hilton Garden Inn Frankfurt Airport | Reported **66% less overproduction**. Winnow also reports **64% less overproduction** at Hilton Vienna Park and **69%** at Hampton by Hilton Budapest.[2] |
| **Hotel food-waste reduction** | Winnow pilots at four Mandarin Oriental hotels | Reported **36% less food waste**, **$207,000 in annualized net savings**, and 66 tonnes of food waste eliminated.[3] |
| **Hotel food-waste reduction** | Leanpath at Sheraton Grand Hotel & Spa Edinburgh | The kitchen reported a **64% reduction in food-waste value** and **58% reduction by weight** over just over a year.[3] |
| **Hotel kitchen waste / overproduction** | Winnow at 13 hotels in the UAE | Reported to have **halved breakfast waste**; the source does not provide a financial ROI figure for this cohort.[3] |
| **Commercial kitchen waste tracking** | Automated tracking systems across HORECA sites in a study | The study found **23–51% reductions** in food waste in most establishments, with cost savings of **1–39%**; results varied by site, and one hotel saw waste rise.[9] |

**How to read these claims:** The Winnow and Leanpath figures are vendor- or operator-reported case-study outcomes, not necessarily independently audited ROI. The HORECA study offers broader measured evidence, but does not establish results for every vendor or deployment.[2][3][9]

The search results did not provide sufficiently substantiated, quantified production outcomes for **hotel or airline overbooking/no-show prediction, restaurant labor scheduling, housekeeping optimization, airline crew disruption recovery, or ancillary-offer optimization**. An Alaska Airlines research abstract describes evaluating cancellation/no-show forecasts in revenue-management simulations, but gives no realized operational ROI, and its evaluation includes pricing—outside the requested scope.[4]
