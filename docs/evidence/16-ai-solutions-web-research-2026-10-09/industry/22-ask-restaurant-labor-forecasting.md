The strongest publicly quantified examples in the available sources are **Carrot Express** and **an unnamed fast-casual restaurant chain using Legion**. Cabo Bob’s also reports a sales-forecast accuracy result, but not a quantified labor-cost or food-waste improvement.

| Company | Forecasting vendor | What was forecast and how it was used | Published measured outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Carrot Express** | **Lineup.ai** | Sales demand, translated into labor needs and schedules across 17 restaurants; the case study says its machine-learning forecast incorporated internal and external factors. | Payroll reportedly fell by about **300 basis points**; the company projected **$1.1–$1.3 million in annual labor savings**. The savings figure was described as expected/annualized, not as a completed audited result.[3] |
| **Unnamed fast-casual restaurant chain** | **Legion** | Location-level demand forecasts used to optimize labor and generate schedules; Legion says machine-learning models incorporate real operating data. | Team members received **10% more hours per week on average** (reported as 22 to 27), **overtime expense fell**, and absenteeism decreased **5%**. The chain is not named, and the source does not quantify the overtime reduction.[2] |
| **Cabo Bob’s** | **Lineup.ai** | Sales forecasts informed suggested labor hours and scheduling; the system considered historical sales and external factors such as events and weather. | The company said forecasts were within **2–3% of actual sales** and that labor had become more manageable and predictable. It did **not** publish a labor-cost percentage, sales-per-labor-hour, or food-waste reduction.[4] |

**Bottom line:** These sources support measured labor or forecast-accuracy results, but they do not establish a published food-waste outcome or sales-per-labor-hour improvement for these examples. The figures are vendor-published case-study claims, so they should be treated as reported customer outcomes rather than independently verified benchmarks.[2][3][4]
