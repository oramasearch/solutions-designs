The answer in one line
The most valuable pipelines sit on high-frequency business decisions with a closed loop from prediction → action → measured outcome. That's why McKinsey finds ~75% of generative AI's $2.6–4.4T annual value potential concentrated in customer operations, marketing/sales, software engineering, and R&D (McKinsey).

Tier 1 — build these first
Personalization / next-best action — 5–8% revenue lift, 20–30% lower cost to serve (McKinsey); Netflix values recommendations at $1B+/year in avoided churn (Yahoo Finance)

Demand forecasting & supply-chain optimization — 20–50% forecast-error reduction, up to 65% fewer lost sales (McKinsey)

Customer support automation — worth 30–45% of function cost; Klarna's assistant did the work of 700 agents with a $40M profit impact (Klarna)

Software engineering copilots/agents — 20–45% of engineering spend; 55.8% faster task completion in controlled trials (Microsoft Research)

Fraud, risk & anomaly detection — Mastercard detected 3x more fraud with 10x fewer false positives (AWS)

Tier 2 covers pricing, document intelligence, predictive maintenance, churn prediction (rank 9), and sales opportunity mining; Tier 3 covers R&D acceleration, cybersecurity, procurement leakage, manufacturing yield, finance close, and workforce planning.

Where churn fits
Your example is a strong Tier 2 case — a 5% retention gain can lift profits 25–95% (HBR), and one industrial supplier saved $200M/year with churn prediction (dotData). But it's best built as one branch of a broader next-best-action platform: predict who's worth saving, why they're at risk, which intervention works, and avoid discounting customers who'd stay anyway.

What makes a use case maximally valuable
Seven traits: large economic pool, high decision frequency, closed feedback loop, an action layer (not just predictions), reliable data exhaust from core systems, measurable ROI via A/B tests or hard operating metrics, and reusability as a platform across use cases.
