## Response (verbatim)

````
Production use is moving from “search a content library” to **AI-generated, source-linked first drafts with human approval**. Teams connect the agent to approved past answers, product documentation, and security/compliance materials; it drafts RFP/RFI, due-diligence and security-questionnaire answers, flags gaps or low-confidence items, and can populate portals or proposal documents. People still review factual claims, tailor win themes, and approve submissions. Reported gains are mostly vendor- or customer-reported, not controlled evidence that AI alone caused higher win rates.

## What the metrics usually mean

- **Time per RFP:** A cited 2025 industry summary puts the average at about **25 hours**, with AI-tool users reporting responses in **under five hours**—roughly 20 hours saved per proposal. Results depend on RFP length, how current the answer library is, and how much review is required.[4][5]
- **More RFPs with the same team:** AutoRFP.ai’s utility example reports 14 to 29 annual submissions without adding team members; Perk reports more than twice the response volume.[7]
- **Win rate:** Treat reported increases cautiously. Win rates are affected by deal selection, bid strategy, pricing, and market conditions; the available examples are customer reports or comparisons, not causal trials.[2][5][8]
- **Headcount saved:** The examples surfaced report **hours saved**, increased output with the **same team size**, or improved capacity—not a reliable number of positions eliminated. In practice, the common operational claim is capacity freed for more bids and more proposal tailoring, rather than a documented reduction in headcount.[7]

## Which industries adopt it most?

The clearest adoption signal in the available material is **technology/software**, especially companies that repeatedly answer enterprise RFPs and security reviews. Software response teams are described as leading generative-AI use; security-questionnaire workflows also fit cloud, cybersecurity, and other B2B vendors facing recurring vendor assessments.[5][6]

Other likely high-use areas are **financial services and insurance**, where procurement and compliance questionnaires recur, plus **healthcare, professional services, and public-sector suppliers** that compete through formal tenders. The results available here do not provide a defensible industry-by-industry adoption ranking, however. Evidence is strongest for technology and security-heavy B2B workflows, not for a precise ranking across all industries.[5][6]
````
