# 02 — The problem: the intelligence bottleneck

**Logline:** Every company has the data and the questions. What it doesn't have is enough people who can turn one into the other — so everyone waits in their queue.

---

In most companies, getting from a question ("what's driving churn?") to a real answer means going through the handful of people who can do data engineering, ML, and deployment all at once. They're swamped. The business has been saying the same thing for years: *you're too slow.* The data sources are known, the conversations are recorded — so what's taking so long?

Two gaps cause it. First, the **bottleneck**: every request funnels through a central person or team, and a queue forms — adding people barely helps. Second, the **prototype-to-production gap**: someone builds a quick version in a tool like n8n or a notebook, it kind of works, and then making it *correct, governed, and deployed* turns into a separate multi-month project that never gets prioritized.

So the prototype rots, the question goes unanswered, and the org keeps flying blind on the things that matter most — the churn, the risk, the demand it could be acting on today.

**Talking points**
- The scarce resource isn't data — it's people who can turn data into deployed intelligence.
- Central-team bottleneck → a queue; "you're too slow" for years.
- The prototype (n8n/notebook) proves the idea but never becomes a real, governed system.
- Result: high-value questions (churn, fraud, demand) go unanswered while the data sits right there.

**Lines worth saying**
- "Everyone has the data. Almost no one can turn it into an answer fast enough to matter."
- "The bottleneck isn't your data. It's the line outside the one person who can use it."

**Show (visual beats)**
- A queue of tickets stacking up behind one overwhelmed engineer.
- A slick n8n prototype → a "TODO: productionize" sticky note that never moves.
- A dashboard nobody reads while a churn number quietly climbs.
