# 10-research-computer-vision-midmarket

- **Tool:** `mcp__perplexity__perplexity_research`
- **Query:** What production computer vision solutions do mid-sized companies (not just big tech) deploy with documented ROI in 2022-2026? Cover visual quality inspection in manufacturing, retail shelf monitoring and planogram compliance, workplace safety / PPE detection, construction progress monitoring, insurance and auto damage estimation from photos, yard and dock management (trailer/license plate recognition), food waste tracking in kitchens, agriculture and produce grading, and counting/measuring inventory with drones. For each: the decision or action triggered, data needed (cameras, labeled images), typical vendors or consultancies, and reported results with company names.
- **Timestamp (UTC):** 2026-10-09T20:38:05.409Z

## Response (verbatim)

````
**Yes—these are operational deployments, not just big-tech demonstrations.** The clearest mid-market examples are Federal Package’s production-line inspection, GoGo SqueeZ’s shelf-photo program, RG Construction’s progress tracking, ANA Seguros’s photo-based claims workflow, and Taylor Logistics’s drone cycle counts. The figures below are *reported outcomes*, not independently calculated returns; where a source reports an efficiency or accuracy gain rather than a financial ROI, I label it accordingly.

| Production computer vision solution | Decision or action triggered | Data needed (cameras, labeled images) | Typical vendors or consultancies | Reported results with company names |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Visual quality inspection—packaging** | Reject a leaking or incorrectly labeled unit; route defects for correction rather than shipping them. | On-line monochrome and color cameras; examples labeled **good** and **bad** for the product and packaging in question. | **Cognex** edge-learning vision systems. | **Federal Package**, a contract manufacturer, inspects **100%** of deodorant packages and reports **over 99% defect-catching accuracy**. Training for a new product run takes **5–10 minutes**. The case reports increased throughput, but **no dollar ROI**.[web:307] |
| **Visual quality inspection—assembled hardware** | Flag an emerging assembly defect, contain affected units, and investigate its root cause before release. | Images captured at assembly stations, linked to build history and test results; reviewed examples of defects and acceptable variation. | **Instrumental**. | **Toast** installed **six inspection stations across two programs**. It reports **5.3× ROI** from engineering-cost savings, schedule reductions and improved field performance; its case also describes defect containment on production lines. This is a combined development-and-production return, **not an inspection-only ROI**.[web:270] |
| **Retail shelf monitoring and planogram compliance** | Send a representative to fix missing stock, placement, pricing or display execution; revise store-specific recommendations. | Representative-taken shelf photos; product/SKU reference images and the intended shelf or “perfect store” standards. | **Trax Image Recognition**; **ParallelDots ShelfWatch** is another shelf-image provider. | **GoGo SqueeZ** reports an **11% rise in points of distribution**, **7% sales growth versus the period before Trax**, and **26% year-over-year sales growth**. Outlets adopting its recommendations grew **three times faster** than non-participating outlets; those sales figures should not all be attributed solely to vision.[web:152][web:17] |
| **Workplace safety and PPE detection** | Alert or coach supervisors about missing required PPE or entry into a danger zone; use event trends to change site practices. | Video from existing cameras, a map of restricted areas, and site-specific rules defining required helmets, vests, shoes or gloves; detections need human review where context is ambiguous. | **viAct**, **Protex AI**. | On **Alliad’s Tiassalé hospital-construction project**, viAct reports **41.7% fewer PPE non-compliances** and **46.3% fewer danger-zone intrusions** in three months using existing cameras. Separately, **AB Agri** reports a **99% reduction in measured PPE non-compliance** with Protex AI and **expects**, rather than claims to have realized, payback in under 12 months.[web:225][web:31] |
| **Construction progress monitoring** | Compare installed work with plans and scheduled quantities; reassign crews, correct production problems, substantiate completion and support payment approval. | Repeated site imagery mapped to plans, plus takeoffs, schedule and budget-hour data; AI classifiers identify work such as framing and drywall. | **OpenSpace**; **Buildots** is another progress-tracking provider. | Specialty drywall contractor **RG Construction** says OpenSpace saves **at least 40 hours a month** in remote monitoring and lets it track progress **at least 10× faster**. In one example, a review from a desk took **30 minutes** instead of a roughly **four-hour** site visit.[web:66][web:65] |
| **Insurance and auto-damage estimation from photos** | Identify damaged parts and severity; propose repair versus replacement and an estimate; route a claim or authorize payment for appropriate cases. | Smartphone photos or video of the vehicle, captured by an adjuster or claimant; damage examples and insurer repair-cost and authorization rules. | **Bdeo Visual Intelligence**; **Tractable** is another photo-estimating provider. | Mexico-based **ANA Seguros** reports **40% shorter claims-valuation time**, estimate delivery falling from **72 to 4 hours**, and repairs requesting add-ons falling from **38% to 5%** versus other valuation platforms. It says some indemnity payments moved from **3–5 days to immediate**.[web:177][web:170] |
| **Yard and dock management—plate/truck recognition** | Identify a truck at entry or exit, create its gate record, enrich its vehicle profile and make assets easier to locate; match arrivals to yard workflows. | Gate-camera views of vehicles, license plates and DOT numbers; vehicle/appointment records and exception review. Trailer-number performance should be validated separately from plate performance. | **Terminal Industries**; its camera partnership includes **Flock Safety**. | At **Ryder**, the original **2024 ongoing site pilot** processed **10,000+ truck detections** with **99% accuracy for plates and DOT numbers**. A later Terminal case study reports gate check-in falling from about **14 minutes to under 2**, **90% less manual asset-search effort**, and **projected—not realized—5.7× three-year ROI**. Ryder is a large operator, and the published measurements principally describe its pilot rather than a separately measured full rollout.[web:49][web:188][web:246] |
| **Food-waste tracking in kitchens** | Show chefs what food is being discarded so they change batch sizes, portions, purchasing or use of trimmings. | A camera and scale at the disposal point, food-category recognition, weight readings and a baseline for comparison. | **Winnow Vision**; **Orbisk** offers another camera-based kitchen system. | At the single-property **Hilton Rotterdam**, Winnow reports **25% less food waste in eight months** and **€16,000 in annual food-cost savings**. The case describes concrete changes to pasta portions, sandwich production and reuse of trimmings; its reduction compares a January 2024 baseline with August–October 2024.[web:121] |
| **Agriculture and produce grading** | Grade each item by visible quality and weight, then direct it into the appropriate pack or market stream. | Images of produce on the sorting line, weighing data, and grade/blemish specifications; optical grading equipment and representative fruit examples. | **TOMRA Food Spectrim**; **TOMRA LUCAi** is used for other produce-grading lines. | **Del Rey Avocado** reports **72% higher daily production** after installing TOMRA Spectrim, **without adding floor space**. Its CEO specifically credits precise photo grading and weighing; this is a throughput gain, **not a published monetary ROI**.[web:93][web:91] |
| **Drone inventory—warehouse counting** | Flag pallet counts that disagree with the warehouse-management system, replenish low pallets, and consolidate partly occupied locations; send people to count exceptions. | Autonomous-drone photos of rack locations, visible barcodes and text, pallet images, and WMS expected quantities; validated examples for recognition and count inference. | **Gather AI**. | Mid-sized 3PL **Taylor Logistics** says Gather AI’s inferred-case-count workflow is **87% more efficient** than its team’s physical cycle counting. The system photographs locations and displays inferred counts and occupancy; the **87%** is Taylor’s reported efficiency comparison, not a cash-return calculation.[web:105] |
| **Drone inventory—outdoor stockpile measurement** | Calculate volume and tonnage, reconcile physical stock with accounting records, and adjust production or sales plans before a large inventory write-down. | Overlapping drone aerial photographs, ground-control/GPS data, a 3D surface and stockpile-base model, and material labels and densities; this is **photogrammetric measurement**, not simply counting objects in images. | **Propeller Aero**; **DroneDeploy** also supports stockpile measurement. | Across **Boral’s six Victorian quarries**, Propeller reports survey-data turnaround **within 24 hours**, versus a previous **two-to-three-month** wait for aerial-survey results. Boral reported about **$25,000 inventory variance across the six sites** on its cited survey and uses the measurements for smaller, more frequent stock adjustments. The case does **not** quantify a net ROI.[web:212][web:213] |
````
