# 11 — perplexity_research — mid-market and small-business AI surveys

- **Tool:** `mcp__perplexity__perplexity_research`
- **Query:** "What do mid-market (middle market, $10M-$1B revenue) companies say about AI adoption in 2025-2026? Summarize the RSM US Middle Market Business Index AI survey, the National Center for the Middle Market, Grant Thornton, KPMG, Vistage, and US Chamber / SBA small business AI surveys. Which specific use cases are they deploying, what results do they report, what is their typical budget, what blocks them (data, skills, cost), and do they rely on outside consultants or vendors?"
- **Timestamp (UTC):** 2026-10-09T20:29:37Z (response received)

## Response

````markdown
**The 2025–26 picture is broad adoption but uneven depth:** middle-market firms report using AI for drafting, analytics, forecasting, IT work and customer service, yet fewer have embedded it across core processes. The clearest reported gains are time savings and better insights; data readiness, integration, skills and governance remain the recurring obstacles.[web:33][web:32][web:5kkEa9xmpmhS1uYLWJD4TCKV]

| Survey | Specific use cases and adoption | Reported results | Typical budget | What blocks adoption or scale | Outside consultants or vendors? |

| **RSM US Middle Market Business Index (MMBI), 2025–26** | Among firms planning or considering AI investment, **85%** named employee efficiency or productivity as its primary driver. The MMBI does not itemize deployed applications.[web:106] | **74%** expected to increase AI spending over two years. In the January 2026 cybersecurity survey, only **35%** reported a formal AI-governance framework.[web:106][web:31] | **No AI dollar budget reported** in these MMBI findings.[web:106] | Staffing and skills: among executives expecting difficulty filling openings, **62%** planned or considered training existing staff, **61%** AI investment and **59%** preparation for AI. Governance lags deployment.[web:106][web:31] | **No AI consultant-use rate reported** in these MMBI findings.[web:106][web:31] |
| **RSM Middle Market AI Survey, 2025** | **91%** reported organizational GenAI use, but **25% of users** had fully integrated it into core work. Uses included text generation/summarization (**49%**), workflow development (**45%**), forecasting/demand planning and sales/marketing content (**40% each**); by business function, data analytics (**58%**), IT (**57%**) and customer service (**48%**).[web:33] | Among GenAI users, **50%** reported time savings on IT projects, **45%** on analytics and **39%** on customer service; **88%** said the impact was more positive than expected.[web:33] | **76% of GenAI users had a dedicated budget**, but the survey release gives **no dollar amount**.[web:33] | **92%** encountered rollout challenges. Among those reporting implementation issues, **41%** cited data quality, **39%** privacy/security and **35%** insufficient internal skills.[web:33] | **70%** said they needed outside help to get the most from AI; **47% of those with a dedicated GenAI budget** used it for AI consulting.[web:33] |
| **RSM Middle Market AI Survey, 2026** | **86%** reported AI partly or fully integrated into operations; only **36%** said it was fully embedded in core processes. Reported application categories were GenAI (**73%**), language AI (**64%**) and prediction AI (**61%**).[web:32] | **97%** were satisfied with business value and **54%** said returns exceeded expectations, although **51%** of those who had run pilots described their success as moderate or limited.[web:36][web:32] | **58% planned to invest at least $1 million** in the current fiscal year; **84%** expected spending to rise the following year. This is **not a median for $10M–$1B firms**: the U.S. sample includes organizations with revenue up to **$10 billion** and surveyed organizations already using AI.[web:32] | Top deployment inhibitors: data quality/availability (**34%**), security/privacy (**30%**), legacy integration (**28%**) and skills (**28%**). Among respondents with moderate or limited pilot success, **53%** cited data quality and **47%** integration.[web:32] | **52% currently engaged external consultants/advisors**, **52% bought capabilities from external providers** and **56% formed strategic partnerships with AI providers**.[web:32] |
| **National Center for the Middle Market (NCMM), year-end 2025 and mid-2026; $10M–<$1B revenue** | Reported AI use rose from **83%** at year-end 2025 to **91%** at mid-2026. Mid-2026 applications included drafting/analysis (**53%**), predictive analytics (**49%**) and conversational AI (**41%**).[web:16][web:b5QZQQmdseg1WBDYN0TMmWGc] | **66%** reported positive AI returns in NCMM’s mid-2026 account. In its year-end comparison, AI users averaged **12.9%** annual revenue growth versus **5.8%** for nonusers; NCMM cautions that AI use also tracks broader organizational readiness, so this is **association, not an AI-caused gain**.[web:16][web:0GiEqLH46SbGISjRL6dAhBSr] | **No typical dollar budget reported**. **67%** expected to increase AI spending; in the year-end comparison, **21% of AI users**, versus **6% of nonusers**, chose AI capital expenditure when asked where they would put an *incremental* investment dollar—not what fraction of budgets they spend on AI.[web:16][web:0GiEqLH46SbGISjRL6dAhBSr] | The Center highlights implementation cost, data quality, privacy/security and workforce capabilities as concerns.[web:20][web:26] | **No consultant- or vendor-use percentage reported** in these findings.[web:16][web:20] |
| **Grant Thornton, 2025 Digital Transformation Survey and 2026 AI Impact Survey** | In 2025, **57% of surveyed private companies**, versus **80% of public companies**, had adopted GenAI; common uses were analytics/business intelligence and customer relationship management. Grant Thornton describes dashboard summaries, forecasting, customer-support chatbots and document or invoice generation. Its **$100M–$1B** 2026 subgroup provides the closer midmarket comparison.[web:J18IgQkrjDtoIfSiBUcxgLMW] | In that 2026 midmarket subgroup, **51%** reported improved insights, **36%** cost reduction and **30%** revenue growth from AI.[web:5kkEa9xmpmhS1uYLWJD4TCKV] | **No midmarket AI dollar budget reported**. The 2025 finding that **37% of private companies** planned an increase of at least **11%** concerns **technology spending overall**, not an AI-only budget.[web:J18IgQkrjDtoIfSiBUcxgLMW][web:5kkEa9xmpmhS1uYLWJD4TCKV] | In the $100M–$1B subgroup, **70%** said at least half their core applications were not AI-ready, **29%** named workforce skill gaps a primary obstacle, and only **14%** had implemented a formal enterprise AI strategy in operations.[web:5kkEa9xmpmhS1uYLWJD4TCKV] | **No midmarket consultant-use percentage**; Grant Thornton says larger firms were more than three times as likely as the $100M–$1B group to say they did **not** need external help to scale AI.[web:5kkEa9xmpmhS1uYLWJD4TCKV] |
| **KPMG 2025 U.S. Private CEO Outlook** | CEOs described back-office efficiency and customer experience as AI opportunities; **64%** said they were moving staff into AI-enabled roles. These are private-company findings, **not a dedicated $10M–$1B sample**: surveyed CEOs led firms above **$500 million** in revenue.[web:jkP0UIzNLsp4byjj9OnInd0d] | **82% expected** AI returns in **one to three years**; just **5% expected** them within a year. These are expectations, not realized ROI.[web:jkP0UIzNLsp4byjj9OnInd0d] | **73% expected to allocate at least 10% of their budget to AI**, but the sample extends well beyond the middle market; this should **not** be treated as a typical midmarket budget.[web:jkP0UIzNLsp4byjj9OnInd0d] | About **two-thirds** called data readiness a challenge; **34%** cited implementation cost, **81%** competition for AI talent and **40%** the gap between existing and needed skills.[web:jkP0UIzNLsp4byjj9OnInd0d] | **47%** said they worked with external AI experts to help their workforce adopt AI.[web:jkP0UIzNLsp4byjj9OnInd0d] |

These are reported outcomes among users, **not proof AI caused the increases**.[web:3rkjuHkP61oPO1uIAFQ9xL9Q] | **No AI dollar budget reported**.[web:3rkjuHkP61oPO1uIAFQ9xL9Q] | Among **nonusers**, **33%** cited tool quality, **30%** cost, **28%** legal/compliance concerns, **24%** insufficient knowledge and **16%** employee training or skills.[web:3rkjuHkP61oPO1uIAFQ9xL9Q] | Among AI users, **63%** mostly or entirely relied on AI **developed by other companies**; **8%** mostly or entirely developed it in-house. Buying a vendor’s tool is not the same as hiring a consultant.[web:3rkjuHkP61oPO1uIAFQ9xL9Q] |

**Budget bottom line:** these sources do **not** establish one representative annual AI budget for the requested **$10M–$1B** revenue band. RSM’s **58% spending $1 million or more** is the strongest dollar benchmark here, but its 2026 U.S. sample includes firms up to **$10 billion**; KPMG’s budget-share finding likewise includes larger firms. The Chamber’s **58%** and SBA’s **8.8%** should not be read as conflicting estimates of the same thing: they use different survey questions and measures of AI use.[web:32][web:jkP0UIzNLsp4byjj9OnInd0d][web:3rkjuHkP61oPO1uIAFQ9xL9Q][web:DFpd0Ij4lDLshLgSyo4sMZCp]
````
