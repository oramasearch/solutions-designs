# 28-ask-warehouse-slotting-labor

- **Tool:** `mcp__perplexity__perplexity_ask` (search_context_size: high)
- **Query:** What documented results exist for ML-driven warehouse slotting optimization and pick-path / order batching optimization at mid-sized distribution centers and 3PLs (vendors such as Optricity, Lucas Systems, Easy Metrics, Locus, Manhattan, Blue Yonder)? Quantify picker travel reduction, lines per hour, labor cost. Also what results exist for ML labor standards / engineered labor standards in warehouses?
- **Timestamp (UTC):** 2026-10-09T20:42:16.667Z

## Response (verbatim)

````
**Documented results exist, but the public evidence is uneven.** The clearest figures come from vendor or customer case studies, often reporting productivity rather than isolating travel, labor-cost savings, or the contribution of machine learning. Treat them as site-specific claims, not guaranteed results for a typical mid-sized DC.

## Slotting, pick paths, and batching

- **Lucas Systems:** The company says its AI-based travel optimization, combining batch picking and path optimization, can reduce travel **30–70%**. It reports customers average a **36% increase in picking productivity**; a cited healthcare operation improved from **50–56 to 100–110 lines per hour** in one picking area, while bulk picking improved about **20%**. These are vendor-reported figures, not a controlled comparison of slotting, batching, and routing individually.[10]
- **Locus Robotics:** A food 3PL, Sakalas, reportedly increased pick rates from roughly **45–60 to 90–100 lines per hour**—nearly doubling them—and achieved up to **50% improvement in overall productivity**. The product combines mobile robots with workflow and travel-path optimization; the case does not isolate the effect of software-driven batching or slotting.[3]
- **Easy Metrics:** Outdoor-lifestyle 3PL Dock High Logistics reportedly improved throughput productivity **50%** using cost-to-serve data and labor-management analytics. The public summary does not attribute that gain specifically to slotting or pick-path optimization.[7]
- **Optricity, Manhattan, and Blue Yonder:** The available results here do not provide a sufficiently specific, quantified customer case for those vendors’ slotting or batching tools. Vendor capability claims alone should not be treated as documented site outcomes.

## Labor productivity and cost

- **Easy Metrics / C.H. Robinson:** Easy Metrics reports a **10% performance gain and $450,000 in savings** from ProTrack. The public summary does not specify the measurement period, baseline labor spend, or how much of the savings came from labor versus other operational changes.[1]
- **Easy Metrics / American Tire Distributors:** Its case-study listing reports **25–30% higher units per hour at select sites during the first year** of a labor-management system; that is a productivity metric, not a stated labor-cost reduction.[1]
- **Locus / Motivational Fulfillment:** A case study reports **61% higher productivity** and **20% lower manpower**. It describes a robotics deployment, so those outcomes should not be attributed to ML slotting or labor standards alone.[2]
- **Locus 3PL claims:** Locus advertises **2–3× pick rates** and up to **90% less unproductive walking** for certain deployments. These are broad vendor claims; the specific Sakalas case gives a more concrete line-rate comparison.[3]

## ML labor standards and engineered labor standards

Public evidence is harder to pin down for **ML-based standards** specifically. The results found support the broader use of labor-management analytics, performance visibility, and data-led workforce decisions, but do not establish that machine learning created or continuously recalibrated engineered standards.

- Easy Metrics’ Dock High example reports **near-zero “missing time”** and a **50% throughput-productivity improvement**, tied to performance visibility, cost-to-serve analysis, and incentive pay—not a disclosed ML labor-standard model.[7]
- Easy Metrics’ ATD listing reports **25–30% UPH improvement at select sites** in the first year of a labor-management deployment, but does not say how engineered standards were developed or quantify labor-cost savings separately.[1]
- Lucas says it introduced **machine-learning-based dynamic slotting and predictive workforce planning** in 2021, but the cited results do not isolate the impact of either feature or provide a measured labor-cost effect attributable to ML standards.[10]

**Practical read:** The strongest public, quantified signals are roughly **30–70% less travel** from Lucas’s combined batching/path-optimization claim, near-**2× pick rate** in the Sakalas example, and **10–50% productivity gains** in several labor-management or 3PL cases. These metrics are not directly comparable: sites differ in order profile, baseline, labor mix, automation, and measurement method. For a buying decision, request a customer reference with before/after **lines per paid hour, walking distance, overtime/agency labor, accuracy, and cost per order line**, plus the exact feature set and time window.
````
