Here are documented examples with named organizations and quantified results. The strongest public evidence is for contact-reason analytics, forecasting/WFM, and automated interaction analysis; public case studies are thinner on warranty abuse and CSAT/NPS prediction.

| Use case | Named deployment and measured result |
|---|---|
| **Contact-driver analytics to reduce repeat calls** | **HomeServe + Verint:** analyzed nearly 2 million interactions, found that about 20% of voice contacts were repeat calls, and identified document callbacks and incorrect transfers as major drivers. Process changes reduced service call levels by 10% and average hold time by 20 seconds per call.[10] |
| **Predicting repeat calls and improving FCR** | **A UK ISP + R Systems:** combined speech, CRM, IVR, and call data to identify repeat-call drivers and predict likely callbacks. After recommended process and training changes, the ISP improved first-call resolution by up to 5% and reported $4.9 million in cost savings.[4] |
| **Contact analytics linked to product fixes** | **A home-trades SaaS company + serviceMob:** analytics on a business handling roughly 35,000 monthly contacts identified high-friction product issues for engineering to fix; the company reported a 78% reduction in contact rate.[3] |
| **Forecasting and staffing** | **Oscar Health + NiCE WFM:** doubled call volume with only a 60% increase in staffing, according to NiCE’s customer results.[9] **ScS + NiCE:** reported a 50% reduction in call abandonment and nearly 30% fewer overall contacts; NiCE WFM improved forecasting to support coverage.[6] |
| **Forecasting and schedule optimization** | **A home-trades SaaS company + serviceMob:** the vendor reports 95–97% intraweek forecast accuracy, compared with a stated conventional range of 85–90%; this is a vendor-reported result, not an independently audited benchmark.[3] |
| **Automated analysis of all recorded calls** | **HomeServe + Verint:** Verint Speech Analytics analyzed 100% of recorded calls, helping surface repeat-contact categories and operational causes.[10] This establishes full-call coverage for analytics, but it is not the same as automated QA scoring. |
| **Automated interaction summaries / agent support** | **An unnamed telecom provider + NiCE:** automated summaries captured intents, actions, outcomes, and sentiment across 100% of agent interactions; the company reported 25% less time spent on after-call work.[8] |
| **Call-reason analytics at scale** | **Admiral + Verint:** analyzed calls across three major service departments to categorize reasons for contact and identify customer pain points; the case study reports resource savings from reducing manual call sourcing, but gives no numeric outcome.[7] |

**Evidence gaps:** The available case studies did not provide a named, quantified deployment for **ML-based warranty/returns-abuse detection**, **real-time agent assist with a measured customer-service outcome**, or **CSAT/NPS prediction**. For **100% QA auto-scoring**, the telecom example supports 100% interaction analysis and summaries, but does not specifically claim automated quality scoring of every call.[8]
