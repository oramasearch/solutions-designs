# Enterprise AI Solutions Compendium

## Executive orientation

A **solution** is a methodology applied in a context-correct real-world setup and connected to business action, ownership, systems, measurement, governance, and adoption. It is not merely a use case or methodology. BCG’s 2024 research found 74% of companies struggle to achieve and scale AI value and attributes roughly 70% of implementation challenge to people and process, 20% to technology/data, and only 10% to algorithms ([BCG 2024](https://www.bcg.com/press/24october2024-ai-adoption-in-2024-74-of-companies-struggle-to-achieve-and-scale-value)). McKinsey’s 2025 State of AI emphasizes that organizations capture more value when they redesign end-to-end workflows instead of pursuing disconnected pilots ([McKinsey 2025](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/quantumblack/our%20insights/the%20state%20of%20ai/2025/the-state-of-ai-how-organizations-are-rewiring-to-capture-value_final.pdf)).

**Generic anatomy:** methodology → correctness adaptations → decision policy → action integration → people/process → measurement loop → operational monitoring → governance/safety → adoption loop. Google’s production ML guidance emphasizes metrics, simple first models, infrastructure tests, freshness, silent-failure detection, and skew monitoring, which are why this compendium treats deployment architecture as part of the solution ([Google Rules of ML](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/guides/rules-of-ml); [Google Cloud skew monitoring](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/monitor-models-training-serving-skew-vertex-ai)). Google’s hidden-technical-debt paper warns that ML systems accumulate debt through pipeline jungles, glue code, undeclared consumers, configuration, and feedback loops if they are not engineered as production systems ([Google hidden technical debt](https://research.google/pubs/hidden-technical-debt-in-machine-learning-systems/)).



## A. Customer & revenue


### 1. Churn prediction & retention program

**Name.** 1. Churn prediction & retention program

**Use case solved.** Predict customers likely to churn and trigger retention actions that create incremental saved margin.

**Methodology composed from.** Tabular supervised/survival methodology: feature snapshots → XGBoost/logistic/survival model → SHAP/uplift → ranked worklist/report.

**Correctness adaptations.** Build features as-of score date; label churn only in a future window; exclude cancellation, final-bill, save-offer, or complaint outcomes not available at decision time; handle class imbalance, censoring, CLV, contact fatigue, and temporal splits. Sprint used prospective observation and right-censoring for survival-based churn timing ([Sprint/SAS churn paper](https://support.sas.com/resources/papers/proceedings/proceedings/sugi27/p114-27.pdf)); a 2024 telecom churn study emphasized imbalance handling and XGBoost-style classification ([Scientific Reports churn study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-63750-0)). Point-in-time data, temporal validation, train/serve parity, drift monitoring, and silent-failure checks are default requirements for production ML; Google explicitly calls out metrics-first design, freshness requirements, infrastructure tests, and training-serving skew monitoring ([Google Rules of ML](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/guides/rules-of-ml); [Google Cloud skew monitoring](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/monitor-models-training-serving-skew-vertex-ai)). Temporal leakage is especially dangerous because it lets a model use information unavailable at decision time ([PLOS ONE temporal-leakage taxonomy](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0340167)). Retention labels must exclude cancellation-processing traces, save-offer exposure, and post-score service outcomes. Offer-cost and contact-limit features should be frozen as of the score date so uplift is not confused with propensity. The production test should align with randomized retention holdouts; uplift and incremental retained margin; contact fatigue and complaints. A prelaunch correctness review for Churn prediction & retention program should test whether weekly/daily batch from billing introduces data that would not exist at decision time. It should also verify that the measurement plan, especially randomized retention holdouts, matches the action that the business will actually take.

**Business wiring — actions.** Allocate save-desk calls, loyalty offers, service recovery, price-plan moves, or no-contact; policy combines risk, CLV, uplift, offer cost, contact cap, and eligibility.

**Business wiring — systems of action.** CRM, CDP, billing, campaign management, call-center desktop, offer engine, experimentation platform.

**Business wiring — people & process.** The operating loop for Churn prediction & retention program is owned by Retention marketing, save-desk managers, customer-success, finance, privacy/legal, MLOps. Work runs on weekly or daily scored save queues plus monthly retention review. Keep action in the workflow.

**Human-in-the-loop design.** Agents decide conversation and supervisor approves expensive offers; automate only low-risk nudges. Review for Churn prediction & retention program belongs where policy, cost, safety, or rights can change the outcome. Overrides should be captured with reason codes. Escalation paths should name the owner from Retention marketing who can change policy when overrides cluster in the same segment. Keep review queues within capacity.

**Measurement loop.** Use randomized retention holdouts; uplift and incremental retained margin; contact fatigue and complaints. For Churn prediction & retention program, model quality is not enough; the action must move a business metric in the target population. Compare acted, unacted, and overridden cases. Evidence base: [Sprint/SAS churn paper](https://support.sas.com/resources/papers/proceedings/proceedings/sugi27/p114-27.pdf); [PLOS temporal leakage taxonomy](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0340167); [Predictive Analytics World on Target](https://www.predictiveanalyticsworld.com/machinelearningtimes/target-really-predict-teens-pregnancy-inside-story/3566/). Segment cuts should include the main variants for this pattern, such as telecom/subscription save desk, because average lift can hide harm or weak performance in a regulated or high-cost segment. Use each review to tune the playbook.

**Operational architecture.** Weekly/daily batch from billing, usage, service and complaint snapshots; real-time only at cancellation events. Serving latency, data freshness, and retraining triggers should match the decision window for Churn prediction & retention program. Monitor drift, latency, overrides, and outcomes.

**Governance & safety.** Consent, opt-outs, vulnerable-customer handling, offer fairness, leakage controls define the control boundary. For Churn prediction & retention program, logs should preserve the recommendation, policy version, human decision, action, and outcome. Raise approvals with impact.

**Time-to-value & effort profile.** 6-10 week thin version if billing and campaign systems exist; long pole is offer economics. The smallest useful release should cover one decision, one workflow, one owner group, and one measurable outcome for Churn prediction & retention program. Integration and change work are often critical.

**Adoption & change management.** Shadow against current lists; publish lift by decile; capture agent override reasons. Users need evidence, explanations, and a safe way to disagree with the recommendation in Churn prediction & retention program. Training should include what the system does not know. Train daily users and policy owners together. Track usage, overrides, and cadence change.

**Documented deployments.** Sprint documented survival modeling for telecom churn timing ([Sprint/SAS churn paper](https://support.sas.com/resources/papers/proceedings/proceedings/sugi27/p114-27.pdf)). Telecom research documented ML churn prediction and imbalance controls ([Scientific Reports 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-63750-0)). VodafoneZiggo’s related NBA deployment increased agent use of recommendations 200% and acceptance 114% in five months ([VodafoneZiggo/Pega](https://www.pega.com/customers/vodafoneziggo-marketing)).

**Failure modes.** No funded save action, stale timing, cherry-picked holdouts, leakage from cancellation artifacts, privacy backlash. In Churn prediction & retention program, weak incentives, missing capacity, bad thresholds, or unowned exceptions can erase strong offline accuracy. Relevant cautionary sources include [Sprint/SAS churn paper](https://support.sas.com/resources/papers/proceedings/proceedings/sugi27/p114-27.pdf); [PLOS temporal leakage taxonomy](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0340167); [Predictive Analytics World on Target](https://www.predictiveanalyticsworld.com/machinelearningtimes/target-really-predict-teens-pregnancy-inside-story/3566/). Early-warning signals for Churn prediction & retention program include rising overrides, growing exception queues, stale inputs, and a widening gap between recommendations and completed actions. Ask whether the action still fits the conditions.

**Variants.** Telecom/subscription save desk; SaaS customer-success; banking RM outreach; batch report to CRM queue. Variants change latency, rights, error tolerance, and tools. Treat advisory, embedded-workflow, and automated versions of Churn prediction & retention program as separate maturity stages.

**Build vs buy landscape.** Usually configure CRM/CDP/campaign tools; build proprietary features and offer economics. Workflow, permissions, audit, and integrations are often bought, while thresholds, features, eval sets, and economics for Churn prediction & retention program are configured or built. High-risk settings need deeper customization.

**Key references.** [Sprint/SAS](https://support.sas.com/resources/papers/proceedings/proceedings/sugi27/p114-27.pdf); [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-63750-0); [Google skew monitoring](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/monitor-models-training-serving-skew-vertex-ai)

### 2. Next-best-action / personalization engine

### 3. Lead & opportunity scoring in CRM

**Name.** 3. Lead & opportunity scoring in CRM

**Methodology composed from.** Supervised ranking/classification over CRM, marketing, firmographic, engagement, and product-usage features.

**Correctness adaptations.** Avoid post-contact leakage, deduplicate leads/accounts, use region/product-specific models, handle stale opportunities, and require enough qualified/disqualified history; Dynamics requires at least 40 qualified and 40 disqualified leads and trains from closed leads in a selected period ([Microsoft Dynamics predictive lead scoring](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/sales/configure-predictive-lead-scoring)). Training labels must use outcomes known after the initial sales action, not later rep activity that the model itself will cause. Duplicate leads and account hierarchies need deterministic resolution before scoring, or sellers will see inconsistent priorities. The production test should align with lead/account-level A/B or phased rollout; speed-to-lead, meetings, pipeline, win rate, cycle time. A prelaunch correctness review for Lead & opportunity scoring in CRM should test whether daily or event-triggered CRM scoring introduces data that would not exist at decision time. ### 5. Promotion optimization

**Name.** 5. Promotion optimization

**Use case solved.** Choose promotions that create incremental profitable sales rather than subsidized volume.

**Methodology composed from.** Baseline demand, uplift/causal models, basket/cannibalization, and constrained calendar optimization.

**Correctness adaptations.** Must distinguish promoted-item lift from true incrementality, include halo/cannibalization, availability, vendor funds, and matched holdouts. ## Cross-cutting synthesis 2 — Correctness-adaptation catalog

| Adaptation | What it prevents | How it is implemented | Solutions that require it most |
|---|---|---|---|
| Point-in-time discipline | Leakage from future cancellations, payments, claims, tickets, inspections, or sales stages | Feature snapshots as of decision time; label windows after score time; remove post-action artifacts | Churn, lead scoring, fraud, AML, credit, collections, forecasts, maintenance, AIOps |
| Temporal validation | Random-split overstatement and regime blindness | Walk-forward backtests, out-of-time validation, market/region holdouts | Churn, pricing, demand, sales, cash, load, fraud, maintenance |
| Censoring and delayed labels | Bias from unobserved churn, stockouts, prior collections, maintenance, fraud, or loan performance | Survival models, delayed-label windows, stockout adjustment, reject inference | Churn, demand, collections, credit, fraud, maintenance |
| Entity resolution | Fragmented decisions and duplicate action | Customer/account/household, device, vendor, asset, claim, employee, and account identity graphs | CRM/NBA, KYC, AML, fraud, AP, claims, VoC, asset operations |
| Class imbalance and rare events | Models that ignore fraud, AML positives, defects, failures, security incidents, or churn | Cost-sensitive metrics, PR curves, threshold economics, review queues | Fraud, AML, claims fraud, quality inspection, predictive maintenance, security detection |
| Train/serve consistency | Offline models that fail in production | Shared feature code/store, skew checks, freshness tests, online/offline parity monitoring | All recurring-scoring and real-time systems; Google explicitly calls out skew and freshness ([Google skew monitoring](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/monitor-models-training-serving-skew-vertex-ai)) |
| Cold start | No-history customers, products, SKUs, candidates, documents, or assets | Content features, priors, hierarchy pooling, exploration, manual review | Recommendations, demand, pricing, recruiting, KYC, quality, document extraction |
| Drift handling | Silent decay after market, adversary, product, weather, policy, or process shifts | Data/prediction/performance drift monitors; retrain triggers tied to domain events | Fraud, pricing, forecasts, security, churn, support, maintenance, LLM/RAG |
| Confidence and review thresholds | Unsafe straight-through automation | Field/action confidence thresholds; validation queues; approval gates; audit sampling | AP, KYC, claims, contracts, LLM extraction, agentic automation, security containment |
| Fairness and high-impact governance | Discriminatory or harmful treatment | Adverse-impact/fair-lending tests, protected/proxy review, appeals, human review | Credit, recruiting, attrition, KYC, collections, public-sector risk scoring |
| Grounding and permission trimming | Hallucination or data leakage in LLM systems | Source-cited RAG, ACL filtering, eval harness, refusal policy, tool permissions | RAG assistants, copilots, agentic workflows, legal/contracts, support bots |
| Physical/process safety constraints | Production harm from infeasible setpoints or operations | Safe envelopes, management of change, operator approvals, rollback | Yield, load/energy, quality, predictive maintenance, warehouse, route optimization |

Temporal leakage can make invalid predictions look useful, while train/serve skew can make valid offline models fail at action time ([PLOS temporal leakage taxonomy](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0340167); [Google skew monitoring](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/monitor-models-training-serving-skew-vertex-ai)).

## Cross-cutting synthesis 3 — Methodology → solutions fan-out

| Methodology family | Representative solutions | Why many solutions share it but wire it differently |
|---|---|---|
| Tabular supervised ranking/classification | Churn, lead scoring, fraud, AML triage, credit, collections, attrition, vulnerability prioritization | The model family ranks entities, but action differs: retention offer, SDR call, approve/challenge/block, investigator queue, APR/limit, treatment path, HRBP intervention, or patch ticket. |
| Time-series/probabilistic forecasting | Demand/replenishment, sales/capacity, cash/treasury, load/grid, S&OP reconciliation, workforce scheduling | Forecasts create value only when tied to order, staffing, funding, dispatch, or plan decisions, and each domain has different cost asymmetry. |
| Forecast-then-optimize / constrained optimization | Pricing, promotions, replenishment, route dispatch, warehouse, yield, workforce planning | Correctness requires constraints, capacity, guardrails, and owner approval; the optimizer is not the business process. |
| NLP/document AI/extraction | Ticket triage, VoC, AP invoices, contracts, claims documents, KYC, LLM extraction | Extraction becomes value when fields post to ERP/CRM/claims/CLM/ATS and low-confidence items enter review queues. |
| Computer vision / sensor analytics | Quality inspection, KYC, claims photos, predictive maintenance | Vision/sensor outputs must trigger rework, identity decision, estimate, or work order with physical/process safety controls. |
| Recommender / decisioning / bandit | NBA, recsys, CLV audience allocation, proactive care | Similar ranking machinery fans out into customer contact, product ranking, service playbooks, and budget allocation. |
| Graph/anomaly/security analytics | AML, fraud, security detection, alert correlation, vulnerability prioritization | The output is an investigation, containment, or remediation workflow with auditability and false-positive economics. |
| RAG/LLM/copy/summarization | Enterprise assistants, support resolution, agent assist, copilots, contract review, decision reports | Grounding, permissions, eval, and human acceptance define whether generation is safe enough for workflow use. |
| Agentic tool-use automation | Security containment, AIOps remediation, AP/claims/legal actions, CRM/ITSM updates | Tool access turns suggestions into operations, so approvals, least privilege, rollback, and audit are the core methodology extensions. |

## Cross-cutting synthesis 4 — Action-integration patterns

| Pattern | What changes in the business | Appropriate when | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Report → decision forum | A named committee changes budget, policy, or priorities | Action is strategic or cross-functional | CLV allocation, driver-analysis reports, S&OP reconciliation |
| Ranked queue | Humans work the highest-value/risk items first | Human judgment remains essential and volume is high | Churn save desk, AML investigators, claims/SIU, collections, vulnerability remediation |
| Task in system of action | Model creates an owned task with SLA | Operational team already works queues | CRM lead tasks, CMMS work orders, ITSM incidents, Jira VoC items |
| Policy/decision engine | Output maps to approve/challenge/block/price/route | Decision must be consistent and auditable | Fraud authorization, credit underwriting, pricing/discount governance |
| Review queue / validation station | AI pre-fills and humans validate exceptions | Data extraction or decision confidence varies | AP invoices, KYC, claims, LLM extraction |
| Copilot draft | Human accepts/edits before final action | Knowledge work needs speed with accountability | Agent assist, legal review, developer/seller/service copilots |
| Guarded closed-loop automation | System executes bounded reversible actions | Action is low-risk, frequent, and measurable | Netflix remediation, invoice touchless AP, high-confidence fraud approvals, quality reject gates |

## Cross-cutting synthesis 5 — Measurement-loop patterns

| Pattern | Correct use | Examples | Guardrails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Randomized holdout | Proves incremental effect of acting vs not acting | Churn retention, CLV allocation, proactive care, collections | Customer harm, fairness, contact fatigue, offer cost |
| A/B or interleaving | Compares product/workflow variants | Recommendations, support bots, copilots, agent assist | Long-term retention, CSAT, quality, complaints |
| Champion/challenger | Safely evaluates a new model/rule against incumbent | Fraud, credit, AML, vulnerability prioritization | False positives, false negatives, fairness, manual workload |
| Forecast value added | Tests whether model and overrides improve decision outcomes | Demand, sales, cash, S&OP, workforce | Service level, bias, inventory/capacity/cash buffers |
| Phased rollout | Limits operational risk and learns site/team effects | AP, maintenance, quality, route, warehouse, AIOps | Safety, rollback, site comparability |
| Shadow mode | Builds trust before acting | Security containment, HR, credit, claims, agentic automation | Adoption, false-alarm rate, policy exceptions |
| Audit sampling | Checks straight-through decisions | AP, KYC, claims, fraud, document extraction | Compliance findings, reversals, source evidence |

Netflix’s recommender program shows the need for controlled online experiments, while IBM’s CLV program shows that proof must attach to a changed allocation decision, not merely a better score ([Netflix recommender paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/2843948); [IBM CLV Practice Prize](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.1070.0319)).

## Cross-cutting synthesis 6 — Why solutions fail

| Failure class | Pattern-specific manifestation | Evidence-backed lesson |
|---|---|---|
| No business wiring | Scores and dashboards never create offers, tickets, orders, work orders, or approved actions | BCG and McKinsey identify workflow/process redesign as central to scaling value ([BCG 2024 AI adoption research](https://www.bcg.com/press/24october2024-ai-adoption-in-2024-74-of-companies-struggle-to-achieve-and-scale-value); [McKinsey 2025](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/quantumblack/our%20insights/the%20state%20of%20ai/2025/the-state-of-ai-how-organizations-are-rewiring-to-capture-value_final.pdf)). |
| Invalid correctness setup | Leakage, censored demand, bad labels, train/serve skew, or stale feature pipelines | PLOS documents temporal leakage risks, and Google recommends skew/freshness monitoring ([PLOS temporal leakage taxonomy](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0340167); [Google skew monitoring](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/monitor-models-training-serving-skew-vertex-ai)). |
| Automation beyond safe frontier | Pricing/trading/security/process actions execute without guardrails, rollback, or approval | Zillow Offers lost heavily after price/operations misalignment; Knight Capital lacked adequate safeguards/testing before erroneous orders ([JISE Zillow Offers analysis](https://jise.org/Volume35/n1/JISE2024v35n1pp67-72.pdf); [SEC Knight Capital order](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2013-222)). |
| High-impact recommendations without evidence | Clinical, claims, credit, HR, or legal systems recommend harmful or indefensible actions | STAT reported Watson for Oncology unsafe/incorrect recommendations tied to synthetic training and limited expert bases ([STAT on Watson for Oncology](https://www.statnews.com/2018/07/25/ibm-watson-recommended-unsafe-incorrect-treatments/)). |
| Fairness and transparency failures | Hiring, credit, public-benefit, or workforce tools encode historical bias or lack appeal | Amazon abandoned a recruiting tool that penalized women-associated terms; Dutch benefits risk scoring caused discriminatory harm; Apple Card investigation found transparency/customer-service deficiencies even while not finding unlawful discrimination ([Reuters on Amazon recruiting](https://www.reuters.com/article/world/insight-amazon-scraps-secret-ai-recruiting-tool-that-showed-bias-against-women-idUSKCN1MK0AG/); [Amnesty Dutch algorithm report](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2021/10/xenophobic-machines-dutch-child-benefit-scandal/); [NYSDFS Apple Card report](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/reports_and_publications/202103_report_apple_card_investigation)). |
| Alert/review overload | Triage creates more work or fatigue than it saves | Epic’s sepsis model external validation found low PPV and high evaluation burden, a warning for all alerting workflows ([JAMA Epic sepsis validation](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2781307)). |
| Wrong metric | Teams optimize response, clicks, deflection, MAPE, or usage instead of incremental margin, resolution, service level, or risk reduction | Netflix uses online tests because offline/intuitive metrics are insufficient; Klarna reports repeat inquiries and resolution time, not just deflection volume ([Netflix recommender paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/2843948); [Klarna](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/)). |
| Adoption gap | Users distrust, override, or ignore outputs because they are late, opaque, or outside the workflow | Lightspeed’s rollout used sandboxing, training, A/B testing, legal/security review, and hypercare ([Lightspeed/Fin](https://fin.ai/customers/lightspeed)). |
| Technical debt and drift | Model pipelines decay silently after launches, schema changes, or regime shifts | Google’s hidden-technical-debt paper warns about glue code, pipeline jungles, undeclared consumers, and feedback loops ([Google hidden technical debt](https://research.google/pubs/hidden-technical-debt-in-machine-learning-systems/)). |

## Closing synthesis

The deployed winners treat AI as a changed operating system: correct data setup, explicit decision policy, integration into systems of action, owners who act, proof of incremental impact, and safety governance. Stalled pilots stop at model output or demo quality and never cross the integration, measurement, and adoption gap ([BCG 2024 AI adoption research](https://www.bcg.com/press/24october2024-ai-adoption-in-2024-74-of-companies-struggle-to-achieve-and-scale-value); [McKinsey 2025](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/quantumblack/our%20insights/the%20state%20of%20ai/2025/the-state-of-ai-how-organizations-are-rewiring-to-capture-value_final.pdf); [Google Rules](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/guides/rules-of-ml)).
