# 12 — perplexity_ask — Ramp AI Index vendor and category spend

- **Tool:** `mcp__perplexity__perplexity_ask` (search_context_size: high)
- **Query:** "According to the Ramp AI Index and Ramp vendor spend data in 2025-2026, which AI vendors and categories are businesses paying for the most, and which vertical or application-layer AI vendors (not model providers) are fastest growing by number of paying businesses? Include any breakdown by industry or company size."
- **Timestamp (UTC):** 2026-10-09T20:29:37Z

## Response

````markdown
Ramp’s data points to **frontier-model providers as the biggest direct AI subscriptions and token vendors**, while the fastest-growing *application-layer* products are emerging in meeting notes, sales execution, analytics, and AI search visibility. Ramp’s public summaries do not provide a single comparable ranking of total dollars by AI vendor or a full vendor-by-industry/size table, so the strongest comparisons available here are paid-business adoption and adoption growth.

- **Most widely paid AI vendors:** Anthropic and OpenAI lead. In Ramp’s June 2026 Index, 41% of sampled businesses paid Anthropic and 39.5% paid OpenAI; in July, Anthropic reached 43.5% and OpenAI 39.7%.[3][4]
- **Spend categories:** Ramp tracks subscriptions, coding agents, and tokens/APIs separately, but the available results do not show which category captured the most total spend. Ramp does report that the median AI-spending business spent about $11–$12 per employee monthly in mid-2026, while the top 1% spent roughly $7,400 per employee—evidence that spending is highly concentrated.[3][4]
- **Fast-growing application-layer vendors and categories:**
  - **Profound (answer-engine optimization):** adoption reached 40% by the end of Q2 2026, from 6.6% a year earlier; Peec AI rose to nearly 17%, more than tripling.[9]
  - **Granola (AI meeting notes):** reached 20% adoption in about 18 months, with adoption nearly doubling in the latest quarter reported.[9]
  - **AI-native sales execution:** newcomers’ adoption grew an average 5.3 percentage points year over year, while incumbents declined 0.3 points.[9]
  - **Business intelligence:** AI-native newcomers gained about 5 points year over year; incumbents fell 1.3 points.[9]
  - **Product analytics:** newcomers gained 17 points while incumbents lost 4.5 points.[9]
  - **Content creation:** CapCut was the fastest-growing vendor in its category; Descript was also gaining adoption.[9]

These are **adoption rates or changes in adoption**, not raw counts of paying businesses; the available reporting does not supply comparable business counts for these application vendors. Nor does it identify a single fastest-growing vendor across all application categories. Incumbents still held most category spend: over 70% in sales execution and product analytics, and nearly 83% in business intelligence.[9]

Ramp’s reports cover businesses across sectors including manufacturing, health care, technology, and finance, and the AI Index supports industry and company-size filters.[1][5] But the results available here do not include enough subgroup figures to say which industries or company sizes pay the most, or which application vendors lead within each group.
````
