## Comprehensive Analysis

### High-confidence findings (where convergence = reliability)

The three models agree on the spine of the strategy, and that agreement is your safest ground. **Run a small, paid, tightly-scoped first cohort of roughly 3–6 design partners**, drawn from **late mid-market to lower-enterprise regulated firms** rather than the largest logos. All three independently reached this from the same operational reality: enterprise infrastructure partners are extremely high-touch (Allston Labs' ~10–15 founder hours/week/partner figure recurs in two of the three reports), and a paid pilot — priced at roughly **10–25% of expected ACV and credited toward conversion** — is the right instrument for mission-critical software that touches sensitive data, identity, audit, and deployment ([GrowthX](https://growthx.com/blog/founder-guide-pilot/), [Allston Labs](https://allstonlabs.com/library/design-partners/case-for-design-partners)). Scope discipline is non-negotiable across all three: **one narrow predictive use case, one data domain, one deployment environment, one measurable KPI**, time-boxed to roughly 12–16 weeks.

On verticals, the convergence is striking: **financial services / banking (#1) and insurance (#2)** are the unanimous top two, with **healthcare / life sciences** a unanimous close third. The logic is identical across models — your core primitives (own-data training, in-environment deployment, auditability, model validation, approval gates) map almost one-to-one onto GLBA safeguards and SR 11-7 model-risk expectations in banking ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security/gramm-leach-bliley-act), [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR1107.htm)), the NAIC AI Model Bulletin in insurance ([NAIC](https://content.naic.org/insurance-topics/artificial-intelligence)), and HIPAA plus the highest-in-industry breach costs in healthcare ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/index.html)). All three also converge on the **IP split** (customer owns data + customer-specific weights; you own the engine and generalized learnings), **no broad exclusivity**, and the identity of your real champion: **the prototype-to-production owner** — Head of ML Platform, MLOps Lead, AI Platform Lead, or VP Data Engineering — paired with a business sponsor, not the CDAO alone. Treat these as settled.

The **anti-ICP** is equally unanimous and worth internalizing as hard filters: skip AI-tourist innovation labs with no production owner, SMBs without platform teams, prospects who really want an LLM copilot or RAG search, anyone demanding you take possession of raw data in your cloud, and direct federal/classified deals as a *first* cohort. Finally, all three validate **cloud-cost / repatriation / FinOps pressure as a genuine buying trigger** — framed as intelligent workload placement, not an anti-cloud crusade ([Flexera](https://www.flexera.com/about-us/press-center/new-flexera-report-finds-84-percent-of-organizations-struggle-to-manage-cloud-spend)).

### Where they diverge (and how to weigh it)

The disagreements are mostly about emphasis and precision, not direction. On **program structure**, Claude Opus proposes a formal two-ring model (a 3–4-partner deep-build inner ring plus a 4–6-partner monthly "validation council"), while GPT-5.5 and Gemini favor a single small active cohort backed by a larger discovery bench. These are the same idea expressed differently — the practical resolution is GPT/Gemini's: **3–6 paid active partners now, a 20–40 account discovery bench feeding them, and expansion to 8–12 only after deployment patterns repeat.** Adopt Opus's "validation council" only if you have spare founder bandwidth, since it adds relationship overhead.

The **#4–5 vertical ordering** is genuinely unsettled: Opus ranks logistics/fleet above telecom and manufacturing; GPT puts manufacturing fourth; Gemini puts telecom fourth. They differ because they weight data-density and edge/on-prem urgency against regulatory intensity slightly differently. This divergence is low-stakes — all three agree these three verticals (manufacturing, logistics/fleet, telecom) are a high-fit *second wave* below the FS/insurance/healthcare top tier. Pick whichever of the three gives you the strongest *warm intro*, not whichever ranks marginally higher.

The most consequential divergence is **the wedge thesis**. Claude Opus argues the smartest *non-obvious* first deployment is **industrial / logistics / utility predictive maintenance** — because it validates on-prem/edge deployment and heterogeneous compute with far lower decision-liability than credit denial or clinical diagnosis. Gemini instead argues the wedge is **"policy-to-pipeline translation"** — making governance and evaluation the core of the product — and backs it with the strongest single data point in the entire council: Databricks' finding that organizations using AI-governance tooling move **>12x more projects to production**, and evaluation tooling ~6x more ([Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/blog/enterprise-ai-agent-trends-top-use-cases-governance-evaluations-and-more)). These are not contradictory — they answer different questions. **Use both:** lead your *positioning* with the governance/production-gap wedge (Gemini), and choose a *low-liability use case* for the first technical proof (Opus). Start partners on human-reviewed prediction/triage/forecasting, never autonomous high-stakes decisions.

On **firmographics**, take Gemini's tighter, lower band (≈1,000–25,000 employees, $500M–$10B revenue) as your default targeting filter because access speed matters most pre-PMF, but keep GPT's higher ceiling for the occasional warm-intro lighthouse. On **scorecard mechanics**, the differences (100-point weighted vs. 0–3 rubrics) are cosmetic — all three gate identically on "no disqualifier + a strong, budget-adjacent champion," so use whichever format your team will actually fill in.

### Unique insights worth banking

A few single-model contributions materially improve the plan. GPT-5.5's warning to **ship exactly one reference architecture per environment class** (regulated cloud VPC, Kubernetes private cloud, bare-metal/on-prem GPU) before supporting more permutations is the single best piece of execution advice in the council — it directly counters the bespoke-integration death spiral that kills infra startups. GPT also surfaced a **Vancouver-adjacent angle** (TELUS / Canadian telecom under data-residency pressure) that's worth a warm-intro attempt given your location. Claude Opus's **5-phase 120-day program table** is the most directly executable artifact across all three reports — adopt it as your operating playbook, especially the "shadow production" phase that proves lift without unsafe automation. Gemini's research went deepest on two precise filters: **OCC model-risk guidance bites hardest at banks over ~$30B in assets** (a clean targeting threshold), and **67% of enterprise leaders plan to move some AI data off public cloud within 12 months** ([CIO.com](https://www.cio.com/article/4012882/cios-rethink-public-cloud-as-ai-strategies-mature.html)) — a sharper repatriation signal than the generic Flexera number. Gemini also gave you the best *vocabulary* for buyer conversations via IDC's broad "AI sovereignty" framing and Mayo's "Data Behind Glass" federated model.

### Recommendation

Synthesizing the council: **Build a ~50-account target list weighted toward regional/mid-market banks ($30B+ assets), specialty insurers/MGAs, and integrated health systems, plus one operational vertical (logistics/fleet or manufacturing) for a low-liability deployment proof.** Run ~20–30 discovery calls, qualify hard on the shared scorecard, and convert **3–5 paid design partners** — ideally one FS/insurance, one healthcare, and one operational/predictive-maintenance partner — each on a single use case under Opus's 120-day phased structure. Position the product on the **production-gap-plus-governance wedge** (Gemini's data is your proof point), prove it first on a **low-liability use case** (Opus's guidance), and protect your roadmap by **standardizing one reference architecture per environment class** before widening (GPT's guidance). Source through practitioner channels over generic lead lists — MLOps Community, KubeCon/CNCF, NVIDIA GTC, Money20/20, InsureTech Connect, HIMSS, FinOps X — and lead every outreach with one named use case, never a general "AI platform" demo. For the candidate shortlist, named-company tables in all three reports overlap heavily (Capital One, Discover, The Hartford, Travelers, Progressive, USAA, Mayo Clinic Platform, Cleveland Clinic, Kaiser/Optum, Maersk, UPS, J.B. Hunt, Ryder, Verizon, AT&T, TELUS, Siemens/Bosch/Caterpillar, Shell, investor-owned utilities) — treat the *named regional-bank / specialty-insurer / IDN archetypes* as your true inner-ring targets and the famous logos as later lighthouse/validation accounts.
