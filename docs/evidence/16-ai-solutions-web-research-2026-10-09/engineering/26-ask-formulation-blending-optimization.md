## Response (verbatim)

````
**Yes—documented adoption exists, including at large multinationals, but public, quantified savings are uneven.** The clearest evidence is for chemical/materials formulation and industrial blending; feed software is an established category, while food, concrete, fuel, and mid-market case studies often report capabilities or projected benefits rather than audited savings.

| Area / provider | Publicly documented result | What it does—and doesn’t—establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Animal feed — Format Solutions** | Format describes least-cost formulation that selects ingredient combinations meeting nutrient requirements and helps manage ingredient prices and supply constraints.[10] | Strong evidence of a mature use case and commercial product, but the available source gives no quantified customer savings. Datacor describes feed-formulation software as a way to reduce costs and improve efficiency, but likewise provides no customer-specific savings figure.[9] |
| **Chemicals, coatings, materials — Citrine Informatics** | In a polymer-formulation case study, a customer improved targeted properties over 10 months; Citrine says the work considered trillions of possible recipes and reduced measurement/process-variability uncertainty by 58%.[5] | A quantified development result, but not a stated raw-material cost reduction or number of experiments avoided. Citrine lists further formulation cases involving ingredient-cost reduction, solvent blends, and more efficient testing, but the search result does not supply their numerical outcomes.[3][8] |
| **Chemicals — Uncountable / Clariant** | A company post reports that Clariant onboarded more than 800 scientists in under 10 months, with data structured across 35+ sites and 1,000+ users.[1] | Evidence of enterprise-scale deployment and data infrastructure—not, by itself, a measured formulation-cost or R&D-cycle-time saving. |
| **Industrial blending — Gurobi / Tata Steel** | Gurobi says Tata Steel’s coal-blending optimization model is projected to save millions of pounds over the long term.[6] | Quantified in broad terms, but described as a projection rather than realized savings; the result concerns coal blending, not a recipe-development trial. |
| **General optimization — Gurobi** | A Forrester commissioned study reports a 0.4% average performance improvement and $3.9 million in three-year operational savings for a representative composite organization; switching solvers delivered an additional 0.1–0.3% cost saving in one interviewed use case.[4] | Useful evidence for optimization economics, but it is not a formulation-specific or named-manufacturer result. |
| **Food/beverage, concrete, fuel, coatings, metals — other named firms** | The available results did not provide verified, customer-specific savings figures for Aimpoint Digital, Alchemy, or Ansatz, nor clear quantified case studies for concrete mix design, food/beverage reformulation, or fuel blending. | These applications are technically plausible and adjacent to the documented optimization cases, but the evidence here is insufficient to attribute savings to those vendors or sectors. |

**Are mid-sized manufacturers adopting it?** The evidence supports adoption beyond one-off research pilots—commercial feed-formulation software is established, and chemical companies are deploying formulation-data platforms at scale.[9][10][1] But the strongest named deployment evidence found is from large organizations such as Clariant, and the results available here do **not** establish how common adoption is among mid-sized manufacturers or provide a reliable mid-market adoption rate.

**Bottom line:** Public claims are most concrete for improved formulation properties, broader access to experimental data, and projected blending savings. For purchasing decisions, request customer references and comparable before/after metrics—especially raw-material cost per unit, development time, experiments run or avoided, and whether savings are realized or projected.
````
