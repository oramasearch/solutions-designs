## Response (verbatim)

````
**Short answer:** ML-based labor forecasting and automated scheduling are in commercial use, including among multi-location operators, but the public evidence is uneven. The strongest quantified result in the available sources is a **Legion/Forrester composite retail case**; many other figures are vendor claims or software-listing summaries, not independently verified, head-to-head results. Publicly comparable evidence for grocery, hospitals, labor cost as a percentage of sales, and named mid-sized chains is especially limited.

## Documented and reported outcomes

| Vendor | Publicly reported result | Evidence caveat |
|---|---|---|
| **Legion** | A Forrester Total Economic Impact study based on interviews with Legion users modeled a 15,000-employee retail organization. It estimated forecast accuracy improving from **87% to 96% by Year 2**, with **6–7% fewer scheduled labor hours**; the study also estimated **$50.8 million in three-year benefits** and a **$47.3 million NPV** for its composite organization.[3] | These are modeled composite results based on interviews, not a controlled trial or a single retailer’s audited outcome. |
| **Quinyx** | One secondary software comparison reports **20% less over- and under-scheduling**, attributing the figure to Forrester-based results.[4] | The surfaced source does not provide the underlying study details, customer, or a labor-cost-as-share-of-sales result. |
| **7shifts** | A software listing reports claims of up to **4 hours saved per week** on schedule creation and management, **80% less scheduling time**, and **95% labor accuracy**.[7] | These are platform/listing claims; the source does not establish a consistent sample, definition of “accuracy,” or independent validation. |
| **Fourth / HotSchedules** | The available results describe forecasting and schedule automation capabilities, but do not provide a well-substantiated, quantified customer outcome for 2022–2026.[6][9] | The surfaced quantitative claims come from a secondary 2026 comparison rather than a detailed customer case study. |

**Labor cost as a percentage of sales:** The available sources do not establish a reliable, vendor-attributed change in this metric. A restaurant-sector article cites broad operator reports of **8–15% labor-cost reductions**, but its summary does not identify comparable customers or isolate each named vendor, so it should not be read as a validated result for Legion, UKG, Quinyx, Fourth, 7shifts, Lineup.ai, or Workforce.com.[9]

## Is this adopted by mid-sized multi-location chains?

**Yes, adoption is plausible and the tools are marketed for multi-site operations—but the evidence here does not quantify mid-sized-chain penetration or establish adoption by named mid-sized customers.** The surfaced material includes restaurant-focused and multi-industry products, while Legion’s quantified Forrester composite is much larger than a typical mid-sized chain.[2][3][6][7] So the defensible conclusion is that multi-location deployment is established as a product use case, but **adoption rate and typical mid-market ROI remain unproven by these sources**.

For evaluating a vendor, ask for results from a similar chain and require definitions for forecast accuracy, “overstaffing,” labor cost, and time saved. Also request before-and-after data that accounts for sales changes, wage increases, seasonality, and service levels; headline percentages alone are not directly comparable.
````
