Commercial and industrial firms use ML forecasting to predict facility load, tariff peaks, weather and market prices, then coordinate batteries, HVAC and production schedules to reduce bills or earn demand-response revenue. The strongest quantified examples in the available sources are Stem deployments; the sources do not substantiate comparable savings for every vendor named below.

| Company or use case | How it works | Reported result |
|---|---|---|
| **Fortune 500 building-materials manufacturer, Ontario — Stem Athena** | A 5.3 MW / 8.5 MWh battery and Athena forecasting software target Ontario’s five annual peak hours, which influence Global Adjustment charges. | Peak demand reportedly fell about 90%, from a facility load above 10 MW to just under 1 MW; reported savings were about **C$1.6 million**. The case page is dated 2026, so treat this as a reported case result rather than an independently verified audit.[1] |
| **Granite Construction — Stem Athena** | Batteries store power overnight and discharge during costly demand periods; storage also enabled a more favorable PG&E tariff. | Stem reported **$860,000 in 2020 utility-bill savings across five sites**, including $85,000 at the French Camp site.[6] |
| **Saint-Gobain — Stem Athena** | A 500 kW / 1,000 kWh battery offsets shift-start demand spikes and can respond to grid signals, without changing production schedules. | Stem reported **$20,800 first-year savings** and estimated **$284,260 over 10 years**.[9] |
| **Albertsons, CSU Dominguez Hills, and other Stem customers** | Stem’s case-study listings include grocery and campus battery projects, using storage to manage peaks and energy costs. | The listings report **$380,000 in 10-year savings** for Albertsons and **$70,000 annual savings** for CSU Dominguez Hills; the available listing does not provide enough project detail to compare assumptions.[10] |

In practice, these programs typically combine several tactics:

- **Demand-charge management and peak shaving:** forecast the facility’s likely monthly or annual billing peak, then dispatch a battery or briefly curtail flexible loads before the peak is set. This can suit plants with shift-start spikes and sites facing demand-based tariffs.[1][6][9]
- **Load shifting:** move flexible operations—such as battery charging, refrigeration defrost, HVAC pre-cooling or EV charging—to lower-price hours, while respecting operating constraints. Granite’s case describes overnight battery charging and peak-period discharge.[6]
- **Demand response and grid revenue:** use storage or controllable loads to respond to grid events, potentially adding payments to bill savings. Saint-Gobain’s case describes grid-signal participation as an additional revenue stream.[9]
- **Procurement and tariff optimization:** forecast prices and load to choose when to consume, store or export energy, and assess whether a different tariff improves the economics. Granite’s PG&E Option S tariff was part of its reported savings.[6]

The named vendors—**GridBeyond, Enel X, CPower, Voltus, Stem Athena, Verdigris, Turntide and BrainBox AI**—span demand response/virtual power plants, storage optimization, metering and building controls. The search results available here support specific quantified examples for **Stem**, but do not establish comparable 2022–2026 savings figures for GridBeyond, Enel X, CPower, Voltus, Verdigris, Turntide or BrainBox AI. Savings also depend on tariff design, baseline methods, battery costs, dispatch limits and whether figures represent bill reductions, incentives or multi-year estimates.
