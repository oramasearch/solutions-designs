- **Tool:** `mcp__perplexity__perplexity_ask` (search_context_size: high)
- **Query:** What are the most common project types that AI and machine learning consultancies and freelance ML engineers deliver for mid-market companies in 2025-2026? Rank by frequency if possible, using sources such as Upwork/Toptal job categories, consultancy service pages, and surveys of AI project portfolios. Include classical ML (forecasting, pricing, churn, recommendation, computer vision quality inspection, route optimization) and LLM projects (RAG chatbots, document extraction, agents, voice agents).

## Response (verbatim)

````
There is **no reliable public dataset that ranks consultancy and freelance project deliveries by exact project type**. The best-supported picture combines marketplace demand signals with service offerings; treat the ranking below as an informed estimate, not a measured market share.

| Estimated frequency | Project type | What the work typically involves |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **LLM workflow automation and integrations** | Connecting models to company apps and processes; automating support, research, and back-office tasks. Upwork reported rising demand for AI integration and agent design, and its job listings include LLM workflow projects.[3][6] |
| 2 | **RAG chatbots and internal knowledge assistants** | Searching company documents and answering questions with grounded responses; often includes permissions, citations, evaluation, and deployment. These are prominent in current consultancy offerings.[6][10] |
| 3 | **Document extraction and processing** | Extracting, classifying, and validating information from PDFs, forms, invoices, and other business documents. This appears in freelance listings and consultancy offerings, often paired with workflow automation.[1][10] |
| 4 | **Predictive analytics and classical ML** | Forecasting demand or revenue; predicting churn, fraud, failures, or credit risk; classification and regression PoCs. Consultancy pages explicitly package these as common projects.[10] |
| 5 | **Model deployment, data pipelines, and MLOps** | Productionizing models, building data flows and APIs, monitoring quality, and maintaining systems. Upwork’s in-demand skills include machine learning and data engineering, while production platforms require these capabilities.[4][5] |
| 6 | **AI agents** | Systems that use tools and multi-step workflows, sometimes with human approval. Upwork reported “AI agent” among its top AI-related search queries and noted growth in agent design.[3] |
| 7 | **Computer vision** | Image classification, object recognition, OCR, inspection, and automated review. These are established consultancy offerings, though available sources don’t show how often they are commissioned relative to LLM projects.[10] |
| 8 | **Recommendation and personalization** | Ranking or recommending products, content, or actions. Upwork describes recommendation as a standard ML application, but the sources here do not quantify project frequency.[2] |
| 9 | **Pricing and revenue optimization** | Demand or price modeling, promotion optimization, and related decision support. This is a plausible classical-ML engagement, but the available sources provide too little evidence to rank it confidently. |
| 10 | **Route and scheduling optimization** | Vehicle routing, dispatch, workforce scheduling, and other operations-research projects. These are more specialized and likewise lack comparable frequency data in the available evidence. |
| 11 | **Voice agents** | Speech-based customer-service or operational agents, including transcription, voice generation, and integration with telephony. Current evidence in the available sources is too thin to estimate their rank reliably. |

**How to interpret the ordering:** The clearest 2025 signal is momentum toward generative AI. Upwork reported 25% year-over-year growth in AI-related work volume in Q1 2025 and 52% growth in prompt engineering; its 2025 skills list also included machine learning, data extraction, data engineering, deep learning, and generative AI modeling.[3][4] Its marketplace pages show substantial AI and ML job activity, but their categories overlap and listings are not a representative census of mid-market projects.[1][6]

Thus, the practical split is likely **LLM applications and integrations at the top of current freelance demand**, alongside enduring classical-ML work—especially forecasting and risk/prediction—and foundational data engineering and deployment. The precise ordering of classical use cases such as pricing, recommendations, vision inspection, and route optimization cannot be established from the available sources.
````
