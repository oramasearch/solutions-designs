# 16-ask-voice-receptionist-booking

- **Tool:** `mcp__perplexity__perplexity_ask` (search_context_size: high)
- **Query:** What documented results exist for AI voice agents that answer inbound calls and book appointments for home services companies (HVAC, plumbing, roofing), auto repair shops, dental and medical clinics, property management and restaurants in 2024-2026? Give named businesses or vendors (e.g. ServiceTitan, Avoca, Sameday, Retell, Vapi, Hostie, Slang.ai) with metrics like booked jobs recovered from missed calls, after-hours revenue, cost per call, and conversion rate.

## Response (verbatim)

````
Documented results are **uneven**: the strongest public figures are vendor- or platform-published home-services cases. Restaurant and healthcare sources more often report broad revenue claims, adoption, or company growth—not audited call-level booking results. The figures below are reported claims, not independently verified comparisons.

| Sector | Vendor/business and reported result |
|---|---|
| HVAC, plumbing, and other home services | **ServiceTitan / Bill Joplin’s Air Conditioning and Heating:** ServiceTitan reports more than 1,300 calls handled in just over three months, with over 90% resulting in booked jobs and 72% completed without human intervention.[4] |
| HVAC | **Avoca / Aire Serv Granbury:** Avoca says its booking rate stayed above 90% through peak summer after the office team shrank from three people to one; calls could be booked into ServiceTitan or handed to a staff member.[4] |
| HVAC and plumbing | **Avoca / Yost & Campbell:** Avoca reports revenue grew 20% year over year, primarily from calls it captured that the business had previously lost. Avoca also publishes customer-reported booking figures ranging from 85% to 90%; these are testimonials, not a standardized study.[9] |
| HVAC and plumbing | **Avoca / My Plumber Plus:** A trade-industry roundup reports AI-handled calls booked at a rate 17% higher than calls handled by the company’s prior overflow vendor, across more than 1,000 calls.[10] |
| Home services | **Avoca:** The same roundup reports an average 27% booking-rate lift after six months and 45% payroll savings, but does not provide the underlying sample or calculation methodology.[10] |
| Home services | **Sameday:** Its site advertises a 92% booking rate, without a named customer, time period, or detailed methodology in the available result.[7] |
| HVAC and plumbing | **Toronto contractor case study:** A 2026 case study reports 63% of inbound calls completed by voice AI without handoff; first-contact booking rose from 41% to 67%. It also reports fewer no-shows and more same-day jobs, but does not name the contractor.[8] |
| Restaurants | **Hostie:** Hostie’s materials cite additional revenue of $3,000–$18,000 per month per location for AI hosts. The available result does not identify the restaurants or explain how the revenue was attributed to the agent.[6] |
| Dental and medical clinics | **Patientdesk.ai:** A 2026 roundup reports growth from $17,000 to $50,000 monthly recurring revenue in eight weeks for its dental front-office product. That is vendor growth, not a measured clinic-level appointment or revenue outcome.[2] |
| Property management | **EliseAI:** A 2026 roundup reports the company exceeded $100 million in annual recurring revenue and serves communications for one in eight U.S. apartments. These are company-scale figures, not a call-agent booking or cost-per-call result.[2] |
| Auto repair | **No quantified named-shop outcome surfaced** in the available results. |
| Roofing | **No quantified named-roofer outcome surfaced** in the available results. |
| Slang.ai, Retell, and Vapi | **No comparable, named-customer booking or revenue metrics surfaced** in the available results; this does not establish that none have been published. |

**Bottom line:** For a business evaluating vendors, ask for call-level denominators and definitions: calls answered, eligible calls, bookings made, cancellations, completed jobs, incremental revenue, human transfers, and cost per handled call. The strongest figures above are vendor-published case claims; they should not be compared as if they came from a common controlled study.
````
