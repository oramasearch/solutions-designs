````markdown
The public evidence is uneven: a few jurisdictions have disclosed purchase amounts or pilot metrics, but most reported outcomes are vendor claims, targets, or early-stage results—not audited before-and-after measurements. **Denver’s CivCheck proposal is the clearest disclosed price; Austin’s Archistar deployment has the clearest reported usage and review-time result.**

| Jurisdiction and tool | Purchase / status reported | Results reported |
|---|---|---|
| **Denver, Colorado — CivCheck** | A proposed five-year contract of **$4.6 million** advanced from committee in February 2026; council approval was still pending in the cited coverage. The city’s intended role is applicant-facing pre-submission checks, with staff retaining final review.[1][10] | Denver’s stated goal is to raise first-round acceptance to **80%**, from **37% before the tool**—a target, not a measured result. Rollout was planned for 2026.[1] |
| **Austin, Texas — Archistar eCheck** | A city memorandum says the automated assessment tool was implemented for residential customers in September 2025.[7] A later account reports a three-year contract at about **$1.1 million per year**.[9] | That account reports **190 submissions**, approximately **50% less staff review time**, and no negative customer feedback. It does not provide a comparison period, resubmittal rate, or backlog measure.[9] |
| **Honolulu, Hawaii — CivCheck** | Reported as launched for residential permits in December 2025; no contract value is given in the available sources.[9] | A later account reports a **70% reduction in residential plan-review time**, attributed to Clariti’s announcement. | **Naples, Florida — Blitz AI** | Reported as a city partnership; a later article gives a **$514,096 license**.[8][9] | A reported **85% drop in resubmission rates** appears in a secondary account, but the available results do not establish the measurement period, denominator, or whether the change is independently verified.[8] |
| **Pueblo County, Colorado — Blitz AI** | Reported as grant-funded through Colorado’s Department of Local Affairs, with access free to applicants for the first year; no contract amount is reported.[8][9] | No quantified review-time, resubmittal, or backlog result is reported in these sources. |
| **Harris County, Texas — vendor not selected** | The county reportedly approved an AI-permitting initiative in November 2025, but the cited reporting says no vendor or firm timeline had been selected.[5][8] | No deployment results or contract value reported. |

**Other named products:** The available results do not substantiate a separate government purchase of **Clariti** apart from Clariti’s acquisition of CivCheck, nor do they establish a jurisdiction-level Symbium contract with price and measured outcomes. A secondary article says Bakersfield processed over 500 permits through Symbium, but gives no contract value or independently documented comparison of review time, resubmittals, or backlog.[2][4]

**Bottom line:** Disclosed evidence supports a handful of early deployments, not a reliable cross-city comparison. No cited jurisdiction reports a measured backlog reduction, and resubmittal results are especially sparse; treat percentage improvements as preliminary or vendor-reported unless the city publishes its methodology and baseline.[1][7][8][9]
````
