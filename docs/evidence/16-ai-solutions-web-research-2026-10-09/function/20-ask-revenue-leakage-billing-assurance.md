**The public evidence is much thinner than the headline “1–5% recovered” claims suggest.** The available results contain one named utility case with a dollar recovery but no revenue denominator, and no verifiable named deployments reporting measured recovered revenue as a percentage of revenue across telecom, SaaS, logistics accessorials, or professional services.

| **Utilities — UK water retailer** | A provider reports **£6 million in additional cash** and a 35% year-on-year recovery uplift from collections. The result is not attributed to billing-leakage detection or ML, and no revenue denominator is given.[2] | **Not disclosed; not directly comparable** |

**Bottom line:** I would not present a cross-sector “recovered revenue as a percent of revenue” benchmark from these sources. They do not establish that ML caused the recovery, disclose a consistent revenue denominator, or distinguish identified leakage from cash actually recovered. The utility case is the only named billing example here with a recovery amount, and it still cannot support a percentage.[1]

For a defensible comparison, require each case study to disclose: the named customer and scope; baseline annual revenue; leakage identified versus cash collected; the measurement period; whether recovery is gross or net of implementation costs; and what portion is specifically attributable to ML.
