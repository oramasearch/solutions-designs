# 32-ask-process-setpoint-optimization

- **Tool:** `mcp__perplexity__perplexity_ask` (search_context_size: high)
- **Query:** What documented results exist for ML-based manufacturing process optimization: yield improvement, scrap reduction, energy and raw-material optimization, and setpoint recommendations for process industries (cement, chemicals, food processing, plastics, pulp and paper, metals) using vendors such as Seeq, Sight Machine, Fero Labs, Braincube, Imubit, Basetwo, TwinThread, Canvass AI? Name customers (including mid-sized plants) and quantify savings.
- **Timestamp (UTC):** 2026-10-09T20:42:48.027Z

## Response (verbatim)

````
Publicly documented, plant-level figures are limited and uneven: the clearest quantified example in the available evidence is Fero Labs’ steel case at Gerdau. Other cited results include food-processing savings attributed to Seeq, but most evidence does **not** establish that ML-generated setpoints directly caused the savings. Treat vendor case studies and platform marketing as reported outcomes, not independent verification.

| Vendor / customer | Industry and documented result | What the evidence supports |
|---|---|---|
| **Fero Labs — Gerdau** | At a steel plant, the company reports **$3 per ton of steel** saved in raw-material costs, **15% lower quality variation**, and **500,000 lb of alloy** avoided. It says Gerdau later expanded the software to five other North American plants.[1] | A specific raw-material optimization case: the system recommended lower-cost alloy additions heat by heat while meeting quality requirements. The source does not quantify scrap reduction or energy savings at those plants.[1] |
| **Seeq — Chobani** | Seeq reports **$10,000–$20,000 in weekly savings** in one processing area.[3] | A named food processor and quantified savings, but the public summary does not break down the savings into yield, scrap, energy, ingredients, or setpoint changes.[3] |
| **Seeq — refinery, customer unnamed** | Microsoft’s case study says a refinery reduced the time needed for an analysis from **four months to 30 minutes**.[4] | This quantifies analytics speed, not operating savings or a measured yield/energy improvement; the refinery is not named.[4] |
| **Fero Labs — customer portfolio** | An investor profile names **Covestro, Gerdau, and Henkel** and reports more than **$20 million in savings across more than a dozen plants**, along with nearly a million pounds of raw materials avoided across customers.[7] | Portfolio-level claims, not an allocation of savings by customer, plant, or process. The profile also describes emissions reductions, but does not provide customer-by-customer audited results.[7] |

**What this means for the requested industries and metrics**

- **Metals:** Gerdau is the strongest documented example here, with quantified alloy-use and quality-variation results. Fero describes scrap-mix, defect, and energy use cases, but the cited material does not report measured savings for those specific Gerdau applications.[1][5]
- **Food processing:** Chobani has a named, quantified weekly-savings claim, but no public breakdown of the source of savings.[3]
- **Chemicals:** Covestro and Henkel are named Fero customers, but the available result does not attribute a specific quantified plant outcome to either one.[7]
- **Cement, plastics, and pulp and paper:** The available sources do not provide a named customer case with quantified plant-level yield, scrap, energy, raw-material, or setpoint savings.
- **Seeq, Braincube, Imubit, Basetwo, TwinThread, and Canvass AI:** The available search evidence is insufficient to substantiate named, quantified plant-level results for the requested measures beyond the Seeq examples above. That is a limit of the evidence at hand, not proof that no such cases exist.

For diligence, ask vendors for the **plant name and site scope**, baseline and measurement period, savings calculation and validation method, whether recommendations were advisory or closed-loop, and results separated into yield, scrap, energy, and raw-material use.
````
