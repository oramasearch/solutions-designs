### What the metrics do—and don’t—show

- **Automation rate is not accuracy.** A system may code accurately when it produces a code, yet route a substantial share of charts to human review. Ask vendors to define automation as *charts finalized without human touch*, specify the denominator, and report results by specialty and payer.
- **Hard customer evidence is limited.** The results available here contain few named-customer case studies with baseline, measurement period, and comparable outcomes. Arintra’s cost reduction and Nym’s radiology-denial claim are vendor or comparison-source claims, not enough on their own to forecast results for a physician group or clinic.[4][8]
- **Days to bill and revenue uplift are particularly under-documented** in the available results. “Faster cash,” “higher collections,” or “faster clean claims” should not be treated as measured outcomes without a named customer, baseline, and defined metric.[8]

### Pricing takeaway

Do not assume these products are priced as a percentage of collections. The available evidence points mainly to **custom enterprise pricing**, with some third-party estimates or market commentary describing per-chart models; it does not establish a standard vendor rate card for the five companies.[5][7][9] Percentage-of-collections pricing appears in broader medical billing and outsourced-coding arrangements, but the available results do not verify it as the standard pricing model for these named AI vendors.[1][3]

For a quote comparison, ask each vendor to price the same scope and disclose whether the fee is per chart **submitted**, per chart **successfully coded**, a platform subscription, or a hybrid. Also request the human-review rate, implementation and integration fees, minimum volume, and any performance-based component.
