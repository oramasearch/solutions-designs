Documented deployments with quantified results are strongest for **autonomous negotiation and sourcing optimization**. The available cases are much thinner for spend classification, should-cost/benchmarking, invoice-to-contract leakage, and maverick-spend detection; avoid treating vendor-wide claims as verified customer outcomes.

| Use case | Named deployment and reported result |
|---|---|
| **Tail spend / autonomous negotiation** | **Honeywell + Pactum:** more than **$500 million** in spend run through the platform and **2,500+ negotiations**; one negotiation reportedly saved **$65,000** with no buyer involvement, and supplier engagement exceeded **80%**.[6] |
| **Autonomous negotiation** | **Walmart + Pactum:** **82%** of suppliers in a payment-terms campaign reportedly preferred negotiating with Pactum over a human. That is a supplier-preference metric, not a quantified savings result.[9] |
| **Sourcing optimization** | **Unnamed major beverage company + Keelvar:** **€15 million savings in 2024**, a **62%** reduction in time to launch sourcing events, and 143 workdays saved across 13 events. The company is not named in the published case.[1] |
| **Sourcing optimization** | **Unnamed industrial manufacturer + Keelvar:** reported **20% savings in annual ocean-freight RFPs versus prior bid rounds** and a **40%** reduction in time spent managing data and processes. The company is not named.[2] |
| **Logistics sourcing / cost avoidance** | **Unnamed global technology company + Keelvar:** reported **$60 million cost avoidance in 12 months** after shifting logistics sourcing to a dynamic market-based approach. The public summary does not name the company.[10] |

**What these cases do—and don’t—establish:** Pactum’s Honeywell example documents negotiation scale and a specific savings instance, while Keelvar’s examples document event-level or category-level sourcing outcomes. Several of the largest Keelvar figures are vendor-published but anonymized, so they do not meet a strict “named company case study” standard.

The available results do **not** substantiate named, quantified deployments for **ML spend classification/spend cubes, should-cost modeling or price benchmarking, invoice-versus-contract price compliance, or maverick-spend detection**. Those may be commercial capabilities, but without a named customer, defined measurement period, and attributable savings figure, they should not be presented as documented savings cases.
